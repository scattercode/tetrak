Much of the world's cultural history is looked after by small, independent
libraries and community archives. They often hit two problems at once:
off-the-shelf archive software can't read or search their scripts, and the
systems that can are priced for large institutions.

We work on both. We publish free, open-source tools that turn scans of old
printed text into searchable text, starting with Armenian, and that make it
possible to search across scripts, so someone who types a name in Latin
letters finds the record written in Armenian, Georgian, Cyrillic or
Syriac. And we work with collections teams directly: assessing
what they hold, building and hosting search sites for their catalogues, and
helping with the technical side of grant applications.

## The tools

Free, open source, and yours to use without us.

<div class="grid cards">

-   **[Tetrak OCR]({{< relref "/ocr" >}})**

    Turns scans of old print into searchable text, on your own machine. It
    runs several OCR (optical character recognition) engines over each file,
    keeps the best reading, and sets aside anything it cannot trust for a
    person to check.

-   **[Armenian text recognition]({{< relref "/armenian-ocr.md" >}})**

    A recogniser we trained ourselves for printed Armenian, in both the
    Eastern and the Western language and either spelling. Free to install on
    its own, and the engine Tetrak OCR uses for Armenian pages.

-   **[Transliteration and cross-script search]({{< relref "/translit.md" >}})**

    Works out which script a piece of text is in, writes it in Latin letters
    by the library standards, and reduces every spelling of a name to one
    form so that a single search finds them all. Armenian, Georgian, Russian
    and Syriac.

</div>

## Services

The tools are free and you can use them on your own. When you would rather
have us do the work, we build and host search sites over catalogues, with
searches that work across scripts and spellings; look at what a collection
holds and what would make it findable; write the technical side of grant
applications; and train collections teams on the tools.
