Everything on the [products]({{< relref "/products.md" >}}) pages is open
source: install it, run it, change it, and never speak to us. The services
below are different. They are work we do for you, for a fee, around those
tools and around the systems you already have. Nothing here is required to
use the tools.

## Catalogue search sites and hosting {#catalogue-search}

A public search site over your catalogue, on your own domain, that we build,
keep up to date and run for you.

Your records stay where they are. We pull them in automatically from the
catalogue system you already use, so your staff carry on cataloguing exactly
as they do now, and the site follows along. What changes is what a visitor
can do with them:

- **Searches work across scripts and spellings.** A reader who types a name
  in Latin letters finds the record written in Armenian, Georgian or
  Cyrillic, whichever romanisation the cataloguer used, and the other way
  round.
- **Name matching finds every record for a person**, whichever of their
  names or spellings was recorded: the Western and Eastern forms, the
  pre-reform and the modern, the names a scholar is known by in other
  languages.
- **Galleries and highlights**, so the site can show the material you want
  seen rather than only answering queries.
- **Transcripts beside the images**, as your digitisation produces them, so
  the full text of a scanned item is searchable too.

We host it, watch it and update it. You get a site that looks like yours
and is found by search engines, and your catalogue stays the catalogue of
record.

## Collection assessment and advice {#advice}

Before any software, a look at what you hold: how it is catalogued, in which
scripts and spellings, what has been scanned and what has not, and what
would make it findable by the people you want to reach. We write up what we
find in plain language, with the options and what each would involve, so
that a board, a funder or a volunteer group can decide with the facts in
front of them. Sometimes the answer is one of our tools; sometimes it is a
setting in the system you already have.

## Grant applications {#grants}

Digitisation and access bids stand or fall on their technical and workflow
sections: what will be scanned, how the text will be recognised, where the
results will live, who will maintain them, and what it will cost over the
life of the project. We write those sections with you, cost the technical
work honestly, and can be named in the application as the technical partner
who will deliver it. We cannot promise that a bid will be funded; we can
make sure the technical case is sound and that a reviewer finds nothing to
doubt in it.

## Training {#training}

Hands-on sessions for collections teams using the tools: running Tetrak OCR
over a batch of scans and deciding what to do with the triage queue; adding
transcripts to a catalogue or an Omeka site; romanising and searching
across scripts. Pitched at archivists and librarians, not developers, and
built around your own material where you can share it.
